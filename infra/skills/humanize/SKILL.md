# Humanize — write like a person, not a robot

Prathap's rule, in his words: *"your emails and linkedin posts are not human
enough, you are putting - instead of , and more robotic."*

Two sins to kill on sight:
1. **The dash used as a comma.** Stop it.
2. **Robotic AI vocabulary.** The words below are banned.

Apply this to every email and every LinkedIn post. Run all three passes before
anything leaves.

---

## PASS 1 — Fix the punctuation and structure

### The dash rule (his #1 complaint)
- **Never use an em-dash ( — ) or a hyphen ( - ) where a comma, full stop, or
  "and"/"because"/"so" would do.** This is the single most common AI tell.
- Bad:  *"We reviewed the account — the balance is clear — please pay by Friday."*
- Good: *"We reviewed the account. The balance is clear. Please pay by Friday."*
- If you catch yourself reaching for a dash, use one of these instead:
  - a **comma** for a small pause,
  - a **full stop** for a new thought,
  - the words **and / but / so / because** to join two ideas.
- A hyphen is only allowed inside a real compound word (e.g. "point-form",
  "month-end", "co-pilot"). Nowhere else.

### Sentence rhythm (the other robot tell)
- **Vary sentence length.** Robots write sentences that are all the same length.
  Humans mix short punchy ones with longer ones.
- **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence has three clauses stitched together,
  break it into two or three.
- **Cut the wind-up.** Delete throat-clearing openers. Start with the point.
- **No perfectly balanced "not only X but also Y" / "X, Y, and Z" triples** on
  every line. Real people are messier.

## PASS 2 — Ban the AI vocabulary

Delete or replace these on sight. If a word below survives, the pass failed.

**Banned words / phrases (replace with the plain word in brackets):**
- delve / dive into  → look at, go through
- leverage  → use
- utilize  → use
- facilitate  → help, run
- robust  → strong, solid
- seamless / seamlessly  → smooth, works cleanly
- streamline  → simplify, speed up
- optimize  → improve, tidy up
- holistic  → whole, full
- synergy / synergies  → (just say what actually combines)
- cutting-edge / state-of-the-art  → new, latest
- game-changer / game-changing  → big deal, matters
- unlock / unleash  → open up, get
- empower  → let, help
- navigate (a challenge)  → deal with, work through
- foster  → build, grow
- landscape (the X landscape)  → the market, the area
- realm  → area, field
- testament to  → shows, proves
- underscore / underscores  → shows, points to
- pivotal  → key, important
- myriad  → many, lots of
- plethora  → plenty, too many
- resonate  → land, connect
- elevate  → lift, raise, improve
- tapestry / journey (as metaphor)  → (delete, say the real thing)
- in today's fast-paced world  → (delete entirely)
- it's worth noting that / it's important to note  → (delete, just say it)
- moreover / furthermore / additionally  → also, and, plus
- in conclusion / to summarise  → (delete, just end)

**Banned openers on email/LinkedIn** (already in his house style, repeated here):
- "Good day", "I hope this finds you well", "Hope you're doing well",
  "Kindly note", "I am reaching out to", "I wanted to reach out".
  Open with the person's name and the point.

**Banned LinkedIn tics:**
- No emoji-bullet walls (🚀✅💡) on every line. One emoji max, only if it fits.
- No "Here's the thing:" / "Let that sink in." / "Read that again."
- No fake hook + one-line-paragraph-stacking for reach.
- No hashtag soup at the end. Two or three real ones, or none.

---

## PASS 3 — Read it aloud

Read the whole thing as if speaking to the person across a desk.
- If a sentence is one you'd never actually say out loud, rewrite it.
- If it sounds like a press release or a chatbot, rewrite it.
- Contractions are fine and human ("we've", "you'll", "I'll") — use them in
  emails and posts unless it's a formal legal letter.
- Leave one small human touch: a plain thank-you, a direct opinion, a short
  real sentence. Perfect and polished reads as fake.

---

## Quick self-check before sending (all must be YES)
1. Zero dashes standing in for commas or full stops?
2. Zero banned AI words survived?
3. Sentences vary in length, one idea each?
4. Would a real person say this out loud?
5. Still matches Prathap's house style (name at top, point form, plain English,
   "Regards, Prathap Ganesharajah, CFO")?

If any answer is NO, run that pass again before it leaves.

## Worked example

**Robot version:**
> Good day Team — I wanted to reach out to leverage this opportunity to
> underscore the pivotal importance of our robust new month-end process — it
> will streamline reporting and foster seamless collaboration across the
> business — moreover it is a testament to our commitment to excellence.

**Humanized:**
> Team,
>
> Quick note on the new month-end process. It cuts our reporting time and makes
> the hand-offs between teams a lot cleaner. Please start using it from this
> month.
>
> Thanks for the effort on this.
>
> Regards,
> Prathap Ganesharajah, CFO
